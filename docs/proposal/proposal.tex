\begin{abstract}
This project improves the detection of small infrared UAVs in cluttered backgrounds. Starting from YOLO26n, we study two changes: replacing a shallow convolution with pinwheel-shaped convolution (PConv) and adding a high-resolution P2 prediction branch. Experiments on SIRST-UAVB compare their effects on recall, false positives, and inference speed. The selected model is then tested on infrared videos and deployed on a Raspberry Pi with H10 acceleration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and related work}
Small infrared UAVs have little texture and low contrast, making them difficult to separate from birds and bright background points. YOLO26 supports end-to-end prediction and small-target-aware label assignment. Its lightweight YOLO26n model is the starting point for studying shallow features and prediction resolution\cite{jocher2026yolo26,ultralytics2026docs}.

PConv uses directional sampling to enlarge shallow receptive fields\cite{yang2025pconv}. EFLNet combines adaptive losses, normalized Wasserstein distance (NWD), and multi-level features for infrared detection\cite{yang2024eflnet}.

TY-RIST studies high-resolution heads and inference cost\cite{atrash2025tyrist}. RDANet, DNANet, ISNet, and ACM explore ways to preserve weak-target information during feature extraction\cite{liu2026rdanet,li2023dnanet,zhang2022isnet,dai2021acm}. This motivates our P2 experiment: retaining more spatial detail may help detect small UAVs, but also increases computation. NWD offers another view of tiny-box localization\cite{wang2021nwd} that informs our error analysis.

\section{Research objectives}
This project compares shallow PConv and a high-resolution P2 branch to find which improves small-UAV recall and reduces false positives across target sizes and backgrounds, while measuring the additional inference cost.

\section{Datasets and preparation}
The main dataset is SIRST-UAVB, with 3,000 real infrared images, 2,955 UAV boxes, and 1,742 bird boxes\cite{yang2025pconv}. Training targets a single-class UAV detector, and the bird annotations support false-positive analysis. Preparation checks image dimensions, box coordinates, and class mappings, then creates training, validation, and test splits by source sequence.

IRSTD-1k (1,001 real images), NUDT-SIRST (1,327 synthetic), and SIRST (427 real) provide separate experiments on generic infrared targets\cite{zhang2022isnet,li2023dnanet,dai2021acm}. Native boxes are used where available, and target masks become enclosing boxes after checks on touching and very small targets.

SIRST-V2, DenseSIRST, and NUDT-SIRST-Sea provide possible extensions to complex backgrounds, dense targets, and maritime scenes\cite{dai2023oscar,xiao2025bafe,wu2023mtunet}. Samples are grouped by sequence or original large image, with duplicate checks across sources, including the overlap between SIRST and SIRST-V2.

\section{Proposed approach}
All models use aspect-ratio-preserving preprocessing to 640 by 640 pixels. B0 is the YOLO26n baseline, retaining its heads, losses, and label assignment\cite{jocher2026yolo26,ultralytics2026docs}. B1 replaces one shallow convolution with PConv\cite{yang2025pconv} to examine directional sampling. B2 adds a stride-4 P2 branch beside P3/P4/P5 to predict from a finer feature map.

B1 and B2 are each compared with B0, followed by ablations on module placement and combined configurations. Validation accuracy, tiny-target recall, and inference cost guide model selection.

\section{Experiments and evaluation}
Training runs on a cloud GPU for 300 epochs from the same COCO-pretrained source. Models share data splits, augmentation, and input size, and new-layer initialization is recorded. Checkpoints are selected by validation mAP@0.5:0.95 and evaluated on the same test set. Paired random seeds measure consistency across runs.

Detection quality is measured with precision, recall, F1, mAP@0.5, and mAP@0.5:0.95. Results are grouped by target size in the original image and by background complexity, with missed UAVs, bird false positives, and localization errors inspected separately. Confidence and matching thresholds are chosen on validation data.

Efficiency tests use the same prediction branch and output settings, with nested workloads of approximately 25\%, 50\%, and 100\% of the evaluation frames. Each run records frame counts, runtime, FPS, p50/p95 frame latency, and peak memory. Timing covers decoding, preprocessing, inference, coordinate restoration, and display alongside model latency, and queueing and dropped frames account for the limits during video processing.

\section{Video validation and deployment}
The selected detector is tested on independent infrared UAV videos, including distant targets, bird interference, bright background points, and targets entering or leaving the frame. Annotated frames and continuous segments evaluate detection performance.

The deployment stage takes the model through floating-point inference, ONNX export, quantization, compilation, and video inference on the Raspberry Pi/H10 platform. Calibration images come from the training set. Comparing the deployed model with the floating-point model shows the effect of quantization on detection quality and speed.
